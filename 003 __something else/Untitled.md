## Five ways to ask better questions

## 1. Define the outcome, not just the topic

The gap: asking what you want to know instead of what you want to accomplish.

Basic: "How can I ask better questions?"

Stronger: "Help me improve my prompts so I get more accurate answers, save time, and make better decisions."

Why it matters: Your desired result determines what information is relevant and what a successful answer looks like.

## 2. Give context that changes the answer

The gap: assuming the model knows your situation, experience, or constraints.

Basic: "Help me learn AI."

Stronger: "I'm a beginner who uses AI for studying and personal projects. I have 20 minutes a day and want practical skills rather than technical theory. Build a learning plan."

Why it matters: Context helps tailor the answer to your circumstances instead of producing generic advice.

## 3. Specify constraints and quality standards

The gap: leaving "good" undefined.

Basic: "Write me a business plan."

Stronger: "Create a one-page business plan for a low-cost online service. Include startup costs, target customers, the biggest risks, and a 30-day validation plan. Separate assumptions from facts."

Why it matters: Constraints reduce irrelevant detail, and quality standards make the result easier to evaluate.

## 4. Ask for critique, alternatives, and trade-offs

The gap: treating the first plausible answer as the right answer.

Basic: "Is my idea good?"

Stronger: "Evaluate my idea as a skeptical expert. Identify the three weakest assumptions, give the strongest counterargument, compare two alternatives, and tell me what evidence would change your recommendation."

Why it matters: You get a way to test your thinking, not just reinforcement of it.

## 5. Treat the first answer as a draft

The gap: using one prompt for a task that would benefit from refinement.

Basic: "Give me a study plan."

Stronger: "Create a study plan, identify where it is likely to fail, ask me to test its assumptions, and revise the plan based on my answers."

Why it matters: Iteration lets you correct misunderstandings, add missing information, and improve the result before using it.
